Deere Reports Net Income of $1.051 Billion for Fourth Quarter, $4.860 Billion for Fiscal Year

●Focused execution drives structurally improved results cycle over cycle
●North America leads moderate improvement in large ag as global markets begin to stabilize
●2027 company outlook reflects evolving conditions across key end markets

​

MOLINE, Illinois (November 25, 2026) — Deere & Company reported net income of $1.051 billion for the fourth quarter ended November 1, 2026, or $3.89 per share, compared with net income of $1.065 billion, or $3.93 per share, for the quarter ended November 2, 2025. For fiscal year 2026, net income attributable to Deere & Company was $4.860 billion, or $17.95 per share, compared with $5.027 billion, or $18.50 per share, in fiscal 2025.

Worldwide net sales and revenues decreased 2%, to $12.115 billion, for the fourth quarter of 2026 and increased 4%, to $47.704 billion, for the full year. Net sales were $10.514 billion for the quarter and $41.292 billion for the year, compared with $10.579 billion and $38.917 billion in fiscal 2025, respectively.

“Fiscal 2026 proved the strength of our Smart Industrial operating model,” said John May, chairman and chief executive officer of Deere & Company. “Even at cyclical lows, we executed with discipline. Our results are structurally stronger compared with the same point in the last cycle, and we delivered them while sustaining investments and continuing to return capital to shareholders. Those investments are paying off for our customers, as digital engagement and technology utilization continue to expand.”

Company Outlook & Summary

Net income attributable to Deere & Company for fiscal 2027 is forecasted to be in a range of $4.50 to $5.25 billion. The forecast includes an estimated one-time, non-cash pension settlement charge of $0.25 to $0.40 billion associated with a voluntary lump-sum payment option for certain U.S. salaried retirees.

“As we enter fiscal 2027, we’re seeing early signs of improvement in large agriculture, led by North America,” said May. “Given continued uncertainty in key end markets, our outlook reflects a wider range of outcomes. Even so, we’re well positioned to capture the opportunities ahead by delivering solutions that improve our customers’ productivity and profitability, creating lasting value for our shareholders.”

(1)Acquisitions

​

In 2026, the company completed several acquisitions to advance the capabilities of its existing technology offerings, including the February acquisition of Tenna LLC (Tenna), a U.S. construction technology company that provides mixed-fleet equipment operations and asset tracking solutions. Tenna was acquired for a purchase price of $439 million, net of cash acquired and was assigned to the CF segment. The company also acquired other businesses assigned to the PPA, SAT, and CF segments for a combined purchase price of $16 million, net of cash acquired. Most of the purchase price for these acquisitions was allocated to goodwill and other intangible assets.

In 2025, the company acquired several businesses to advance the capabilities of its existing technology offerings, providing customers with a more comprehensive set of tools to generate and use data to make decisions aimed at improving profitability, efficiency, and sustainability. In addition, the company acquired the remaining ownership interest of an equity method investment. The combined purchase price consideration for these acquisitions was $115 million, consisting of $101 million cash, net of cash acquired, and $14 million loan forgiven. The businesses were assigned to the PPA, SAT, and CF segments. Most of the purchase price for these acquisitions was allocated to goodwill and intangible assets.

(2)Special Items

Litigation Accrual

In the fourth quarter of 2025, the company accrued losses on unresolved legal matters in connection with a consolidated multidistrict class action antitrust lawsuit of $95 million pretax ($75 million after-tax) which was included in “Selling, administrative and general expenses.” This matter was resolved in 2026.

Impairment

In the third quarter of 2025, the company recorded a non-cash impairment charge of $61 million pretax ($49 million after-tax), primarily related to the trade name and customer relationship assets of external overseas battery operations. Of this amount, $53 million was recorded in “Selling, administrative and general expenses” and $8 million in “Cost of sales.” The charge is presented in “Impairments and other adjustments” in the statements of consolidated cash flows. The impairment resulted from slowing external demand for batteries, which indicated that it is probable future cash flows would not cover the carrying value of the assets.

Discrete Tax Items

In the first quarter of 2025, the company recorded favorable net discrete tax items primarily due to tax benefits of $110 million related to the realization of foreign net operating losses from the consolidation of certain subsidiaries and $53 million from an adjustment to an uncertain tax position of a foreign subsidiary.

Banco John Deere S.A.

In 2024, the company entered into an agreement with a Brazilian bank, Banco Bradesco S.A. (Bradesco), for Bradesco to invest and become a 50% owner of the company’s wholly-owned subsidiary in Brazil, Banco John Deere S.A. (BJD). BJD finances retail and wholesale loans for agricultural, construction, and forestry equipment. The transaction is intended to reduce the company’s incremental risk as it continues to grow in the Brazilian market.

15

The BJD business was reclassified as held for sale in 2024. In January 2025, the valuation allowance on assets held for sale decreased, resulting in a pretax and after-tax gain (reversal of previous losses) of $32 million recorded in “Selling, administrative and general expenses.” The valuation allowance changes are presented in “Impairments and other adjustments” in the statements of consolidated cash flows.

The company deconsolidated BJD upon completion of the transaction in February 2025. The company accounts for its investment in BJD using the equity method of accounting and results of its operations are reported in “Equity in income (loss) of unconsolidated affiliates” within the Financial Services segment. The company reports investments in unconsolidated affiliates and receivables from unconsolidated affiliates in “Other assets” and “Other receivables,” respectively.
